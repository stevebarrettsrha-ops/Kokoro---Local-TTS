Directing the read

    
Kokoro has no emotion switch of its own. This studio reshapes the voice's melody, pace, energy and pauses inside the model before audio is made, so emotions stay clean instead of sounding processed. Small moves sound the most human.


    
Directions

    

      
[sad]Read what follows with this emotion until the next direction.

      
[excited:40]Same, at 40% strength. The gauge sets strength for lines without a number.

      
[default]Back to the emotion picked in the side panel.

      
[pause=800]Silence in milliseconds. [pause=1.5s] also works.

      
[beat]A short 350 ms pause, for timing a punchline or a cut.

      
[voice=george]Switch speaker for dialogue. [voice=default] switches back.

      
[speed=1.1]Speed up or slow down the following text relative to the Pace slider.

      
*word*Lean on a word.

      
[Yahuah](yah-HOO-ah)Say this word like this, just here. Split syllables with hyphens and CAPITALISE the stressed one.

      
[live](liv)Sounds-like spelling for one word. Useful for words like live, read or lead whose sound depends on meaning.

      
[Kokoro](/kˈOkəɹO/)Exact phonemes. Ordinary IPA works too and is converted for you.

    

    
Emotions: neutral, warm, joyful, excited, curious, confident, calm, sad, angry, fearful, dramatic, soft.


    
Pronunciation

    
Kokoro guesses names it hasn't seen from their spelling, and that often goes wrong for Hebrew and other non-English words. Add a word once in the side panel's Pronunciation list and it's fixed in every take. Add Hebrew names loads about 60 Scripture and Hebrew-roots names (Yahuah, Elohim, Qadash, Shamayim, Mishlei and more). You can edit any of them, and ▷ plays a word on its own.

    
"Live" is read as liv unless the next word makes it the broadcast sense (live stream, live music) or it ends a phrase like we go live. Override any spot with [live](lyve).

    
Check line at cursor shows the sounds Kokoro will use, so you can see exactly what it reads.


    
Your own voices

    
In the Voice panel, Add a voice from a recording… makes a voice from 10–30 seconds of a real person speaking. Chatterbox reproduces their sound, accent and delivery. Only use your own voice, or one you have permission to use. Run ./setup-cloning.sh once before using cloned voices. Emotions still work, pace is approximate, and Try another read gives a fresh take if one line comes out wrong.


    
Making the video

    
Click → Video after rendering (or Add to video in Takes) to put a voice on the video timeline. In the Video tab's Voices panel, Add with captions also places the spoken words as on-screen text, timed to the voice. Add pictures and clips under Uploads; they play one after another on the Main track. Hover between two scenes and click + for a transition, and give text and pictures an entrance or exit in the right-hand panel. Then click Export MP4. Your projects and exports are kept in the app's creations/ folder.


    
Writing for video

    
    
      
  • One idea per sentence. Each sentence is rendered and timed separately, which keeps captions readable and makes the timing easy to cut to.
    • 
      
  • Use blank lines for scene changes. They become paragraph pauses, which you can lengthen for B-roll.
    • 
      
  • Use [beat] before reveals. It's shorter than a paragraph pause and lands well before a cut.
    • 
      
  • Fit to length re-paces the read to hit an exact duration, e.g. 0:30 for an ad slot. Pauses are adjusted first, then pace.
    • 
      
  • Captions (SRT and VTT) are written with every take, timed from the model's own word timings. Drop them straight into Premiere, Resolve, CapCut or YouTube.
    • 
      
  • Loudness: −14 LUFS for YouTube and social, −16 for general web, −23 for broadcast. If you're mixing music under the voice, pick −16 and turn the music down, not the voice.
    • 
      
  • Room tone stops gaps going dead-silent, so edits in your timeline don't sound like cuts.
    • 
    


    
Getting natural results

    
    
      
  • Kokoro sounds best on full sentences. Very short lines are merged with their neighbour.
    • 
      
  • Punctuation carries the performance: commas for breath, ellipses for hesitation, question marks for lift.
    • 
      
  • Keep intensity in the green zone of the gauge for narration. The red zone is for characters and comedy.
    • 
      
  • Blend type Delivery keeps your voice's tone but borrows another voice's rhythm and melody, which is a quick way to get a different read from the same speaker.
    •
